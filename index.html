Syndet, not soap.

Traditional soap bars are oils mixed with lye. That makes them alkaline, which is great for hands and rough on hair. A syndet (synthetic detergent) bar uses mild cleansers, like the ones in liquid shampoo, pressed into a solid.

Four steps. No learning curve.

  1. 1

    Wet it

    Get your hair soaked and give the bar a quick dip.

  2. 2

    Glide it

    Run the shampoo bar over your scalp a few times, or lather it between your hands first.

  3. 3

    Rinse, then condition

    Rinse clean. Smooth the conditioner bar down your lengths, wait a minute, rinse again.

  4. 4

    Park it

    Set both bars on the tin, out of the spray, so they drain and stay firm.

Questions

Is this just soap?

No. Soap is made from oils and lye. Drop bars use syndet cleansers, the same kind of gentle cleansers found in liquid shampoo, pressed into a solid bar.

How long does a bar last?

That’s one of the things our beta is measuring. We’ll share real numbers from testers instead of guessing.

Will it work on curly or coily hair?

The launch bars are made for normal hair. A curly and thick hair bar is on our list. Tell us your hair type when you sign up, and it helps us decide what comes next.

Is it safe for color-treated hair?

We haven’t tested that yet, so we won’t claim it. When we have, we’ll say so here.

How do I stop it from getting mushy?

Keep it out of standing water. The draining tin in the Starter Kit lets water run off so the bar dries between showers.
