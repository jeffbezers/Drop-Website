## Stack

## Users

Primary: Gen Z and young millennials (teens, college students, young professionals, athletes, travelers) who care about plastic waste but won't trade away good hair to act on it. Secondary: eco-conscious families. Parents often control purchases for Gen Alpha, so public branding does not target Gen Alpha directly.

Many visitors are skeptical. They've tried a shampoo bar that left hair waxy, dry or tangled, or they assume bars are too rustic or niche. They mostly arrive from TikTok or Instagram content on a phone.

## Product Purpose

Drop makes plastic-free syndet (synthetic-detergent, not soap) shampoo and conditioner bars. The company is pre-launch: no product is for sale yet.

The website's current job is validating demand. It explains the product, collects waitlist emails and beta-tester signups, and runs a "Would you switch?" quiz. Success means a waitlist and beta pool large enough to run the first test rounds. The first milestone is 50 testers, with 20–30 saying they'd buy Drop instead of their usual shampoo and conditioner.

## Positioning

"A shampoo bar that does not feel like a compromise." Drop is high-performance haircare with no plastic bottle, not "homemade natural soap." Its syndet base (gentle, coconut-derived cleansers) is designed to lather and rinse like liquid shampoo, unlike traditional soap bars, which leave hair waxy and rough.

The brand promise is that switching feels easy, cool and actually better, without preaching or guilt.

## Operating Context

- Products are used in the shower. Bars go mushy sitting in water, so a draining tin or soap lift is part of the ritual.
- Many people don't know how to use a bar, so plain instructions ("how it works") are core content.
- Travel matters: one small bar replaces several bottles.
- Planned launch line (a tight hero set):
  - Everyday Shampoo Bar: normal hair, fresh scent, easy lather.
  - Moisture Conditioner Bar: detangles and smooths without heaviness.
  - Starter Kit ("perfect for college"): both bars plus a draining travel/shower mesh bag.
- Possible later lines (not announced): curly/thick, oily scalp, fragrance-free/sensitive, sport/travel, teen starter, mini sample pack.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Pre-launch. No checkout, prices or ship dates. A preorder option comes later.
- The waitlist/beta form backend isn't decided yet (e.g. Formspree, Netlify Forms, Google Form). Until then, forms must be easy to wire up later.
- **Claims are regulated.** Drop is a cosmetic under FDA/MoCRA rules.
  - Allowed (only where true): plastic-free, bottle-free, low-waste, vegan, cruelty-free, sulfate-free, silicone-free, made without parabens or phthalates, coconut-derived cleansers, clean-conscious ingredients, "designed to lather and rinse like liquid shampoo."
  - Never: chemical-free, toxin-free, 100% natural, zero waste (unless proven), hair growth, treats dandruff, heals scalp issues, hypoallergenic, dermatologist-tested (unless actually tested), or any medical claim.
- Formulas aren't final. Ingredient and spec details stay general until a cosmetic chemist finalizes them.
- Pages planned: Home, Shop/Starter Set (waitlist for now), How It Works, Why Syndet / Not Soap, Ingredients, Sustainability, About, FAQ, Waitlist/Beta signup.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Drop**. It carries three meanings: drop the bottle, a drop of water, and a product drop.
- The bar is shaped like a water drop, so the product is the logo. A drop-shaped bar in a dish, shot from above, is the core brand image.
- Public wording: lead with "eco-friendly shampoo bar" and "no plastic bottle". Keep it plain; never use the word "syndet" on the site (say "real shampoo, made into a bar").
- Voice: confident, transparent, useful, a little playful. Never preachy, guilt-driven or "eco but worse."
- Approved lines:
  - "Clean hair. Cleaner planet. No bottle required." (homepage opener)
  - "High-performance haircare. Low-waste by design."
  - "Your shower, minus the plastic."
  - "High-performance haircare. No plastic bottle required."
  - "Clean, high-performing, plastic-free haircare — made for real life."
  - "Plastic-free haircare that actually works."
  - "Shampoo bars for people who never thought they'd use shampoo bars."
- Founder: Gavin Zuniga. His photo goes in the "Our story" section when ready.
- Founder story (approved draft): "I discovered shampoo and conditioner bars and realized that one simple shower switch could change the way people think about everyday waste. But many bar products felt too rustic, too drying, or too niche. I wanted to create plastic-free haircare that felt modern, clean, beautiful, and genuinely effective — something people would actually want to keep using."

## Product Principles

1. Performance first, planet second in the pitch. Lead with how it feels in your hair; the plastic win is the bonus, not the sacrifice.
2. Make the switch easy. Every page lowers the barrier: how to use it, where to keep it, what to expect.
3. Explain what's inside and why, without vague "clean" buzzwords.
4. Claims stay honest. If it isn't tested or true, it isn't said.
5. Identity, not guilt. Using Drop should feel cool, never like a chore or a lecture.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Mobile-first (most traffic comes from social). Copy and imagery should cover a range of hair types and textures, even though the launch bars target normal hair.
